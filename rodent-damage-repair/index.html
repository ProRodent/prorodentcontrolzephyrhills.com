What rodents damage.

Rodent teeth never stop growing, so they chew constantly. They gnaw wood, plastic, wire jackets, duct wraps and insulation. In attics, they tear batts into nests, and in walls they chew pipe wraps and drywall.

Damage can be hidden. A chewed duct leaks cool air, which raises your power bill, and a gnawed wire may show no sign until something fails.

Finding the full extent.

  • Attic: flattened or torn insulation, chewed duct jackets and wire wraps.
  • Walls: gnawed drywall edges and access holes.
  • Garage: chewed door seals, shelving and stored goods.
  • Vents and screens: torn mesh and bent covers.
  • Vehicles: chewed hoses and wire jackets, which belong with a mechanic.

The technician photographs each item and writes a scope that separates safe repairs from items that need another trade.

Order of work.

The sequence is trapping, then sealing, then cleanup, then repair. If you rush repairs, rodents may chew them again. A staged plan avoids waste.

How Florida weather affects repairs.

In Florida's heat, damaged insulation costs more over time because attics get hotter and the AC works harder. Repairing it can bring comfort back. Humidity also means damp insulation can grow mold, so the technician flags any moisture.

A realistic budget.

Small repairs such as a few patched holes or a section of duct wrap cost far less than a full attic redo. The estimate lists each line, so you can approve the urgent items and wait on the rest.

Before you call: dust, droppings and your lungs

The wording paraphrases federal health pages and stays general. Take medical questions to your own doctor.

  • Leave the broom in the closet. Bristles and vacuum exhaust both fling dry droppings into the air you breathe.
  • A room shut for weeks needs about thirty minutes of through-draft before anyone goes in to work.
  • Wear gloves the entire time, then peel them off carefully, wash them if reusable and wash your hands.
  • Keep the liquid coming until nothing looks dry, then walk away for a few minutes.

Repair questions.

Can you fix damaged wiring?

That is an electrician's job. If cables look chewed, stay away from them and hire a licensed electrician, and we will document and photograph what we find.

Why wait until animals are gone?

New material is wasted if rodents are still moving in. Trapping and sealing come first.

Will insurance pay?

Policies differ, and many exclude rodent damage. Ask your insurer, and ask us for photos and a written scope.

What about drywall holes?

Small holes can be patched. Larger sections may need a drywall contractor, and we say so.

How do I know what is safe?

Do not touch damaged wiring. The technician marks areas to leave alone and suggests the right trade.
