A twenty-minute evening lap.

Pick a dry evening and take a flashlight. Start at one corner of the house and walk the full perimeter slowly, looking up and down.

  • Look up for limbs, fronds or wires that touch the roof or soffit.
  • Look at the ground for needles, leaves or mulch piled against the siding.
  • Check vents and the gap where the roof meets the wall.
  • Examine the base of the foundation for burrow holes.
  • Listen at dusk for movement overhead.

Write down what you see. If something looks active, photograph it with a coin for scale.

Trees, limbs and the roofline.

Roof rats use trees the way people use stairs. A limb that touches the shingles or the soffit gives them a direct route to the attic. Trimming limbs back several feet removes that route and costs less than a repair.

Do not climb onto the roof yourself in wet weather. Use a ladder safely from the ground or hire a roofer.

Heat, rain and the attic.

A hot attic can exceed outdoor temperatures, and rodents may prefer a shady spot at the roof edge. Summer rain adds humidity and can soften soffit boards, making them easier to chew. A quick look at the soffits after storms can catch soft spots early.

Rodents settle in fast, so a prompt call usually beats paying for repairs later.

Yard habits.

Before you call: handling waste without raising dust

Agencies largely agree on the cleaning method, so the shared steps are summarized here.

  • Dry waste is hazardous mainly as dust, so no sweeping, no vacuuming and no leaf blower anywhere near it.
  • Prop the doors wide, let the breeze work and come back after roughly thirty minutes.
  • Treat everything soiled as dirty until proven otherwise: gloves on, hands washed after, no exceptions.
  • Disinfect first and collect second, because the soaking time keeps dust from lifting as you wipe.
  • Handle a dead animal only through a barrier, and put it straight into a bag.
  • Watch yourself for a few weeks, and call your doctor if flu-like symptoms or hard breathing appear.

Do pines attract rodents?

How often should I clear debris?
