The yard feeds the house.

Hardly any rodent infestation starts indoors. It begins outside, where a woodpile, shed or thick hedge offers shelter and a feeder or fallen fruit offers food. When numbers climb, some animals look for better quarters, and your attic or garage qualifies.

That is why outdoor control matters. It lowers the pressure on the building, and it makes sealing last longer.

Reading outdoor signs.

  • Burrow holes about two inches wide beside slabs, sheds or fences.
  • Packed lanes in the grass or along a wall.
  • Droppings near feeders, trash cans or pet bowls.
  • Gnawed fruit under trees.
  • Rub marks on fence rails and wires leading to the roof.

Photograph what you find and note where it sits. A technician can often tell the species from those clues alone.

Filling burrows while animals are still using them just pushes them to dig somewhere else. Trapping comes first.

Trapping outdoors.

Enclosed, lockable stations go by burrows, along fences and beside sheds. They are weighted or anchored so wind, pets and wildlife cannot tip them. A technician checks them on a schedule you hear ahead of time.

See rat control for the species most often found in yards, and trapping and removal for the method.

Sheds, decks and outbuildings.

A shed is a rodent apartment when its floor lifts off the ground or its door has a gap. Check the floor edge, the corners and the vents. Close each gap with mesh and sealant, and keep anything soft or edible off the floor.

Decks and porches work the same way. Skirting with a torn corner can let animals in. Keep the area underneath clear of stored material.

Storms and the wet season.

After heavy rain, burrows can flood, and animals look for drier cover. Walk the yard once the ground dries and look for new holes and moved debris. Debris piles can hide fresh burrows, so rake them back from the foundation.

Before you call, safe cleanup in plain steps

Moving calmly keeps fine grit from floating where you breathe.

  • A quick sweep feels harmless, yet it can launch particles that hang in still air for a long time.
  • Open it up, wait thirty minutes and keep your face out of the doorway while it clears.
  • Skin contact with droppings, urine or nesting material is avoidable, and gloves make it so.
  • Dampen every pellet and stain completely and let the liquid stand before touching anything.
  • Tie the bag, bin it and wash your hands once more.
  • Mention rodent contact to your doctor if you become ill within weeks, because we stick to pest work.

Yard questions.

Can I reduce rodents without chemicals?

Yes. Remove food, reduce cover and close access. Those steps lower activity, and trapping handles animals already present.

Is a bird feeder a rat magnet?

The spilled seed is. Put a catch tray under the feeder, or hang it far from the house, and store the bag indoors.

Is a retention pond a problem?

Not by itself, but if your lot backs a pond or drainage area, rodents may travel along its edge. Mention it on the call.

Should I cut back my shrubs?

Keep plants a foot or two off the siding, and trim limbs off the roof. You do not need to strip the yard.

What about citrus or fruit trees?

Fallen fruit feeds rats. Pick it up often and bin it. If your property is near a grove or farm field, mention that too.
