Outbuildings: sheds, workshops, detached garages.

Animals often visit an outbuilding first and work toward the house from there. Own a shed or workshop? Say whether its floor is poured slab or bare dirt, and whether sacks of feed, stacked firewood or old paint tins are kept in there.

Why timing counts in Florida.

Spring brings nesting. Rodents that wintered indoors raise litters inside insulation and wall voids, so a house that seemed calm in March can be busy by May, and an early call keeps cleanup smaller.

Signs in plain language.

Houses, garages, sheds and shops all show these signs. Say which ones match your place and how long you have seen them.

  • Tapping or gnawing in the soffit after dark.
  • Loose insulation pulled around the hatch edge.
  • Droppings across a garage shelf.
  • A hot, scorched odor when a long-parked vehicle is started.
  • Dark oily streaks on a fence rail or a pipe.

Before you call: public health advice, boiled down

Thinking about tidying up before a technician comes? Follow the sequence shown, and stop wherever a step feels unclear.

  • Leave the broom in the closet. Bristles and vacuum exhaust both fling dry droppings into the air you breathe.
  • Prop the doors wide, let the breeze work and come back after roughly thirty minutes.
  • Protect your hands from start to finish, and finish with a proper soap-and-water wash.
  • Saturate the pellets and the surface under them using disinfectant, or bleach diluted in water, and leave it be for about five minutes.
  • Seal the bag, then drop it in the regular garbage.
  • If something feels wrong later, see a doctor and mention the cleanup so they can decide what matters.
