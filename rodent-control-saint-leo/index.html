How a visit is sequenced.

Rats often start in the yard, so we begin with outdoor and yard control, tackling burrows, feeders and compost heaps, and move on to the house through rat control. Every finding is reported, even minor ones, and so is anything that falls to another trade.

Larger buildings and quiet rooms.

Big buildings have attics, storage rooms and quiet corners that go unvisited for weeks. If you manage one, tell us which rooms are rarely used, where deliveries arrive and who holds the keys. Those answers shape the route of the walk-through.

For a private house, the most useful details are the age of the roof as you know it, the garage door condition and whether tree limbs reach the shingles.

Heat, rain and your walls.

A cold spell right after a downpour can send animals into a warm, dry attic. When the noises start, write down the date and the room, because that narrows down where they got in.

Evidence that justifies a visit.

People rarely open with a diagnosis. They say they heard something, smelled something or found a mess, and those plain details are exactly what we need.

  • Tapping or gnawing in the soffit after dark.
  • Loose insulation pulled around the hatch edge.
  • Droppings across a garage shelf.
  • A hot, scorched odor when a long-parked vehicle is started.
  • Dark oily streaks on a fence rail or a pipe.
