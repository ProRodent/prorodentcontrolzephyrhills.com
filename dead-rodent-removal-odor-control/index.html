Why the smell is so strong here.

A dead rodent decays faster in heat and humidity, and the odor can seep through drywall, insulation and vents. In a Florida summer, the smell may become noticeable in a day or two, and it can linger for a week or more.

The best fix is not masking. It is finding the animal and removing it.

Tracking the source.

Start in the room where the odor is strongest. Look in low corners, under cabinets, behind appliances and inside closets. Then think about the attic above, since dead animals often drop into hollow spaces.

  • Walk the house and note where the odor changes.
  • Check near the air handler and ducts, since the system can carry smells.
  • Look for flies, stains or a dark spot on drywall.
  • Listen for buzzing in a wall.
  • Check the garage, shed and any vehicle.

A technician uses the same steps, plus a moisture meter or camera when helpful, to narrow down the spot.

Removal.

Once located, the technician opens the smallest access needed, removes the carcass with gloves and bags it. The surface is wetted with disinfectant, wiped and treated. Following the CDC steps, we never sweep or vacuum dry waste.

Treatment and airing.

Odor products designed for the job are applied to the spot, and the room is aired. Humidity can bring odors back, so the technician may recommend a fan or dehumidifier for a day or two. Soaked insulation is replaced.

Poison and the odor problem.

Poisoned rodents seek water and shelter, and many die inside a wall. That is why we steer people away from loose bait and toward trapping. If bait was used, tell the dispatcher so the technician knows what to expect.

The EPA page linked below describes the limits on rodenticides. Our trapping and removal page covers the alternative.

Before you call, put the broom down

Take a beat on each line before touching anything, because a pause protects your lungs.

  • Hurry less. Fast scrubbing motions raise exactly the dust you want to keep down.
  • Prop the doors wide, let the breeze work and come back after roughly thirty minutes.
  • Wear gloves the entire time, then peel them off carefully, wash them if reusable and wash your hands.
  • Contact time counts, so use plenty of disinfectant and let it sit on the waste before you wipe.
  • Handle a dead animal only through a barrier, and put it straight into a bag.
  • We do not give medical advice, and anyone who feels sick after contact with rodent waste should see a clinician.

Odor questions.

How long will the odor hang around?

That varies with the animal's size and the weather. The smell can persist for days or weeks while the source stays put, and taking the source out ends it.

Will air freshener help?

Only briefly. The source has to be found and removed.

Why are flies suddenly indoors?

Flies gather around a decaying animal, so a sudden wave of small flies in the house can mean a carcass close by.

Can I find it myself?

Sometimes. Trace the smell with your nose, check low spots and wear gloves. When the source sits inside a wall, a technician can pin it down without cutting more than necessary.

Does poison cause this?

Often. A rodent that eats poison can crawl into a wall or duct and die there. That is one reason we avoid loose bait.
