Clues worth a call.

Below are the clues we hear about most. One alone proves little, but three together seldom point anywhere else.

  • Footsteps in the ceiling soon after the lights go out.
  • Tiny dark pellets under the sink or in a pantry corner.
  • Cords, boxes or trim with fresh bite marks.
  • An ammonia-like smell inside a closed room.
  • A mouse or rat darting across the floor in daylight.

Homes beside businesses.

Before you call, do this and skip that

Germs can ride on rodent waste, which is why careful work is slow, wet and gentle. Each point traces back to the CDC pages at the foot of the page.

  • Hurry less. Fast scrubbing motions raise exactly the dust you want to keep down.
  • Before you start, open every door and window you can and give the room around half an hour to air through.
  • Treat everything soiled as dirty until proven otherwise: gloves on, hands washed after, no exceptions.
  • Disinfect first and collect second, because the soaking time keeps dust from lifting as you wipe.
  • One tied-off bag is enough for towels and a carcass together, and a second bag helps if the plastic is thin.
  • Watch yourself for a few weeks, and call your doctor if flu-like symptoms or hard breathing appear.
